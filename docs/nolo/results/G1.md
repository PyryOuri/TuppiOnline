## What this does not establish

The reason matters more than the ruling, because the ruling is now load-bearing.

**Independence is not merely unverified — it is absent.** There is one report.
The charter's stated purpose for the gate was anti-anchoring: a single thread
"will produce a fluent, plausible, wrong framing in about ninety seconds, and
every subsequent step will rationalise it", and convergence from independent
starts is the only cheap evidence against that. That evidence does not exist
here and cannot be reconstructed later, because every context now downstream of
framing 1 has read it.

So framing 1's recommendation is a **working hypothesis adopted for cost
reasons**, not a compared and ranked conclusion. Anything built on it inherits
that, and the parent programme's own record is the caution: roughly fifteen
hypotheses were tested and the majority failed, "including several that sounded
obviously right".

## What replaces it

Nothing replaces the provenance check; there is no substitute for it. What
remains is empirical rather than procedural: **N01–N07 verify framing 1's
concrete claims** — the A/B/C witnesses replayed as fixtures, exact counts
against negative controls, complete supports, certificate verification, a
security search that must find the specific lines, and coverage on positions it
has not seen.

That is a different kind of evidence. It cannot show that a better framing was
missed, which is precisely what the gate was for. It can show that this framing
is wrong, and it is arguably a sharper test of correctness than three opinions
would have been. The honest summary is that the programme has traded evidence
about *provenance* for evidence about *accuracy*, and kept only the second.

If N01–N07 fail to confirm the witnesses, that is not a signal to repair the
framing in place. It is the point at which the missing comparison becomes worth
its cost.
