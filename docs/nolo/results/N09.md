# N09 — Paired forced-NOLO trial: sound, complete, and NOT ESTABLISHED

Task status: **COMPLETE**. Scientific outcome: **INCONCLUSIVE / NOT
ESTABLISHED**. The trial was sound and complete — 512 of 512 four-arm blocks,
zero correctness violations — and the candidate's trick improvement over v2 was
**+0.0098 tricks per deal, 95% bootstrap interval [−0.0039, +0.0234]**. That is
below the preregistered 0.05 threshold and its interval crosses zero. No harm was
detected. Date: 2026-09-06.

Preregistration: [`hypotheses-n09-v2.md`](../hypotheses-n09-v2.md), which
supersedes [v1](../hypotheses-n09-v1.md) on a wall-clock cap only. Both are
preserved.

This measures **forced-NOLO deal play at score `(0,0)`**. It is not a measure of
whole-match strength, of play against humans, or of any other scoreboard.

not reported as one.

## The two primary contrasts were numerically identical

`D_B` and `D_C` produced the **same value on all 512 deals** — same mean, same
interval, same win/tie/loss, same permutation statistic. Team 1's policy did
change Team 0's total in four deals, but it moved `BB→BC` and `CB→CC` by the
same amount each time, so the difference was preserved exactly.

The preregistration warned that the two contrasts share deals and arms and are
not independent replications. On this bank they were not merely dependent: the
second added **zero** information. Quoting both as if they were two results would
be reporting one observation twice, and they are quoted here as one.

## Where the effect actually comes from

Only **11 deals of 512** had any difference between arms at all. The seven that
moved Team 0's total:

| Deal | `D_B` tricks |
| ---: | ---: |
| 39 | −2 |
| 148 | +2 |
| 171, 247, 353, 408, 504 | +1 each |

Net +5 tricks over 512 deals, hence the +0.0098 mean. One deal (353) changed a
deal win, in the candidate's favour; that single deal is the entire +0.0020 win
effect. Deal 39 is the one clear regression, and it is reported rather than
explained away: a certified worst-case improvement can still cost two tricks
against the line the baseline would have taken, because the guarantee is about
the worst case and not about this layout.

## Why the endpoint could not have been met

This is the substantive finding, and it is about the design rather than the
candidate.

The candidate only acts inside the last three tricks with at most 100 feasible
worlds. N07 measured how often that produces a correction: **6 of 256 deals
(2.3%)** carried a score-relevant deviation at a Team-0 seat. A per-deal mean is
bounded above by (correction rate × mean shift), so even if every correction were
worth a full trick the mean could not exceed ≈0.023 — less than half the 0.05
threshold. Clearing 0.05 would need corrections worth more than two tricks each,
which the certified bounds make implausible.

The trial delivered 12 corrections in 12 deals in arm CB, close to the screen's
rate, and the observed 0.0098 sits inside that predicted envelope.

**So endpoint 2 was unreachable before the first card was played.** The 0.05
threshold and N = 512 were fixed in the task packet before the correction rate
had been measured; N07 then measured it, and the two are incompatible. This is a
sizing failure in the trial design, not evidence that the framing is worthless.
The binding constraint is the candidate's **coverage** — three tricks, 100 worlds
— not its decision rule.

Recording that is the point. The next question is not "does this candidate win",
which this trial cannot resolve at this N, but "can the certified region be made
large enough to matter", which needs its own task and its own preregistration.

## Two departures from plan, both disclosed

**The v1 run was abandoned at 90 of 512 blocks and never analysed.** Its 4-hour
cap had been justified from a 6-deal pilot measuring 245 CPU-seconds per block;
the real bank runs at about 490, so completion was impossible and the run would
have reported INCONCLUSIVE on compute alone. Its records are preserved unanalysed
in `work/nolo/n09/trial-v1-abandoned/`. v2 changed the cap and nothing else — no
threshold, seed, definition or arm moved — and the run started again from scratch
on the full bank. The v2 preregistration also discloses the two aggregate counts
observed while monitoring v1, none of which could tune anything. In the event the
completed run took 4.55 hours, so the original cap would have missed by about
half an hour.

**A reporting bug was fixed after the run.** The per-arm aggregation summed
`max_latency_seconds` instead of taking a maximum, turning the slowest decision
into a total. It was corrected and the report re-derived from the preserved
per-block records; every effect, interval, statistic and endpoint verdict is
byte-identical before and after, and only the max-latency figures changed. The
trial itself ran under `src/tuppi_nolo/trial.py` `9ddb4982ba21e601…`, the hash in
the preregistration; the aggregation fix makes it `89e2f7a4059233fb…`.

## Limitations

The result is one candidate, one contract, one scoreboard `(0,0)`, one reference
baseline, and 512 deals. The guarantee behind the corrections holds under a
control model in which the partner is adversarial, so SECURITY_ONLY promises
nothing against an opponent who errs. Roughly one decision in twenty inside the
eligible window went unresolved on budget and was delegated, and the candidate
claims nothing about those.

512 deals gives essentially no power against an effect of this size; that is
stated as a property of the design, fixed in advance, and not as a reason to
doubt the number. **N was not extended and no other seed was tried.** A follow-up
needs its own preregistration with a size justified against the measured
correction rate.

Production adoption, other score states and whole-match validation remain
separate later decisions, and nothing here supports any of them.
