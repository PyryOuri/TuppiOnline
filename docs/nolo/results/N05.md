## What was built

[`src/tuppi_nolo/outcomes.py`](../../../src/tuppi_nolo/outcomes.py) maps each
attainable own-team total to a Team-0/Team-1 split **in the acting seat's
orientation**, prices it through `score_nolo` and `transition_rising_score`, and
labels the resulting claim. The losing side's points never enter the formula and
no expected trick count is ever passed to the scorer.

`score_state_proxy` is the exact rational counterpart of the repository's
`match_transition_value`: `+1`/`-1` terminal, otherwise the signed rising-score
lead over 52 as a `Fraction`. It is a deterministic ordering of post-deal states.
It is not match equity and is not named as though it were.

## Which witness decisions are score-relevant

This is the question the task asks be answered before the coverage screen, and
the answer depends on the scoreboard rather than on the cards.

| Fixture | `(0,0)` | `(40,0)` up | `(0,40)` down | `(48,0)` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — `8S` vs `QD` | **UNIVERSAL** | TIE | **UNIVERSAL** | TIE |
| B — `9C` vs `3C` | **SECURITY_ONLY** | **SECURITY_ONLY** | TIE | TIE |
| C — `KC` | TIE | TIE | TIE | TIE |

**A is the only universal improvement**: `8S` totals `[7]` against `QD`'s
`[7,8,9]`, so its certified upper bound sits at the baseline's all-continuation
lower bound with a strictly better case available. **B is SECURITY_ONLY and
cannot be promoted**: its upper bound of 4 is not under the baseline's lower
bound of 3, however good the worst case looks. **C ties everywhere**, which is
why no universal correction is issued and the baseline survives.

The scoreboard dependence is the substantive finding. A's guarantee is worth a
match-losing difference when its team is down at 40 — `(0,44)` against a terminal
`(0,52)` — and worth **nothing at all** when its team is up, because a down-team
win erases the leader whatever the margin. B is the mirror image: it matters at
love and when up, and prices identically once its own team is down.

## A correction made during this task

The first implementation compared raw score pairs, and reported B at `(48,0)` as
`UNCLASSIFIED` because `(60,0)` and `(56,0)` are different numbers. They are the
same *outcome* — the match is over and the surplus buys nothing — so that was an
overshoot preference of exactly the kind the rules forbid. Claims now compare a
settled outcome class in which terminal states collapse to win or loss, while the
authoritative pair remains available for reporting. Both behaviours are tested.

## B's sensitivity, and what it does not license

After a duck the total is 3 with assumed probability `q` and 5 otherwise; the
take-and-club line yields 4 deterministically. The duck's expectation is `5 − 2q`:

| q | duck | take | preferred |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0 | 5 | 4 | take |
| 1/2 | 4 | 4 | tie |
| 1 | 3 | 4 | duck |

The lines cross at `q = 1/2`. These are **assumed scenario probabilities**, stated
to show what would have to be believed for the duck to be preferred; nothing here
is observed, fitted, or a posterior. At `q = 1` the duck is better in expectation
and the label stays **SECURITY_ONLY** — a favourable assumed probability is not
evidence about worst cases, and the test asserts the label does not move.

C's uniform-reference means (`19/4` and `21/4`) remain reference-only and are not
repeated here as though they were values of the imperfect-information problem.

## Limitations

Labels are relative to one stated baseline and one control model, in which the
partner is adversarial. `SECURITY_ONLY` promises nothing against an opponent who
errs, and `TIE` means these lines price the same at the given score, not that the
decision is uninteresting. Only the three supplied witnesses were classified, at
four scoreboards. Whether any of this helps in play is N07's question.
